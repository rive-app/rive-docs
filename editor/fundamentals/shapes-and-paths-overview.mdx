Every vector in Rive is made of two parts:

- A **shape** decides how the vector looks. It holds the [fills and strokes](/editor/fundamentals/fill-and-stroke).
- One or more **paths** inside the shape decide its geometry.

The shape paints every path inside it, so all of its paths share the same fills and strokes. To give part of a drawing a different color, put that part in its own shape.

## Shapes

When you draw with a [Shape tool](/editor/fundamentals/procedural-shapes), the [Pen Tool](/editor/fundamentals/pen-tool-overview), or the [Pencil](/editor/design/freehand-drawing), Rive creates a shape with a path inside it. In the Hierarchy you see the shape, and its paths are nested under it.

![A rounded rectangle selected on the stage, with its shape layer in the Hierarchy](/images/editor/shapes-and-paths/shape-in-hierarchy.png)

Select the shape to edit its fills and strokes in the Inspector. Moving, rotating, or scaling the shape transforms all of its paths together.

![A character's helmet shape selected, with its fill in the Fills and Strokes section of the Inspector](/images/editor/shapes-and-paths/shape-fills-and-strokes.png)

## Paths

Expand a shape in the Hierarchy to see its paths. Select a path to move, rotate, or scale it on its own, without changing the rest of the shape.

![Expanding a rectangle shape in the Hierarchy to select the path inside it](/images/editor/shapes-and-paths/expand-shape-paths.gif)

A path is one of two kinds:

- **Procedural paths** come from the [Shape tools](/editor/fundamentals/procedural-shapes), such as rectangles, ellipses, polygons, and stars. They keep properties like size, corner radius, and number of points, which you can change and animate in the Inspector.
- **Custom paths** are drawn vertex by vertex with the [Pen Tool](/editor/fundamentals/pen-tool-overview), or freehand with the [Pencil](/editor/design/freehand-drawing). You shape them by [editing their vertices](/editor/fundamentals/edit-vertices).

![A star path selected, with its size, origin, radius, and points in the Star Path section of the Inspector](/images/editor/shapes-and-paths/procedural-path-properties.png)

To edit the vertices of a procedural path, it has to become a custom path. When you start editing its vertices, Rive asks you to convert it. Converting removes its procedural properties, along with any keys on them.

## Combining paths in a shape

A shape can hold any number of paths. To add a path to a shape, drag it onto the shape in the Hierarchy. The path takes on that shape's fills and strokes.

![Dragging a path from one rectangle shape into another, so both paths share the first shape's fill](/images/editor/shapes-and-paths/move-path-to-shape.gif)

With a path selected, the Pen Tool also adds new paths to that path's shape instead of creating a new one.

Where a shape's paths overlap, its [fill rule](/editor/fundamentals/fill-and-stroke#fill-rules) decides which areas are filled, so you can use one path to cut a hole in another.

## Moving between shapes and paths

- Press `Enter` with a shape selected to select its first path.
- Press `Esc` with a path selected to select its shape.

Pressing `Enter` with a path selected [edits its vertices](/editor/fundamentals/edit-vertices).
