title: "Masks"
description: "Hide parts of an object using the pixels of another object, with alpha and luminance mask modes."
---

A mask hides parts of an object using another object, called the mask source. Use masks for soft reveals, fades, and cutouts that follow the transparency or brightness of another graphic.

Masks work like [clipping](/editor/manipulating-shapes/clipping), with one difference: a clip uses the source's shape, while a mask uses the source's pixels. That means a mask follows the source's transparency or brightness, not just its outline.

<Note>
  Masks require GPU Canvas in every runtime. See [Feature Support](/feature-support).
</Note>

## Adding a mask

You can mask a shape, a group, a layout, or any other object on the stage, except a path or an artboard. Masking a group masks everything inside it.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    Select the object you want to mask.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    In the **Mask** section of the Inspector, click **+**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    Click the shape, group, or layout you want to use as the mask source.
  </Step>
</Steps>

By default, the mask source is used only as the mask and doesn't draw on its own, so you don't need to hide it. See [Drawing the mask source](#drawing-the-mask-source).

An object can have more than one mask. Each mask gets its own card in the **Mask** section.

## Choosing a mask source

The first row of a mask's card shows its source. Hover it to highlight the source on the stage, or click it to pick a different one.

A mask source can be a shape, a group, or a layout on the same artboard. It can't be:

- A path on its own. Pick the shape that contains it instead.
- An artboard.
- The masked object itself, anything inside it, or its parent.

When you select an object that's used as a mask source, the **Target For** section of the Inspector shows which object it masks.

If a mask has a warning icon next to its source, the mask isn't being applied. This happens when the source has been moved inside the object it masks, or when a [Draw Order Rule](/editor/animate-mode/animating-draw-order#draw-order-rules) splits the masked object. Hover the icon to see which one.

## Mask modes

The **Mode** dropdown sets which part of the source's pixels decides what shows:

- **Alpha**: Opaque areas of the source show the object, and transparent areas hide it. This is the default.
- **Inverted Alpha**: Transparent areas of the source show the object, and opaque areas hide it. Use it to cut a hole in an object.
- **Luminance**: Bright areas of the source show the object, and dark areas hide it.
- **Inverted Luminance**: Dark areas of the source show the object, and bright areas hide it.

For example, mask an image with a shape whose fill is a white-to-transparent gradient in **Alpha** mode to fade the image out, or use a black-and-white image as the source in **Luminance** mode.

## Turning a mask on and off

Click the toggle at the end of the **Mode** row to turn a mask on or off without removing it. To remove a mask, click its minus icon.

The on and off state, the mode, and **Draw Source** can each be keyed on a timeline and [bound to data](/editor/data-binding/overview), so you can switch a mask on or change its mode during an animation.

## Drawing the mask source

**Draw Source** is off by default, so the source is used only as the mask. Turn it on to also draw the source normally, for example when the source is a visible part of your design that should also mask another object.

## Custom bounds

By default, Rive measures the mask's region from the source's content. Turn on **Custom Bounds** to set the region by hand with its **Position** and **Size**. Use it to crop a mask to a region, or for content that can't report where it paints, such as a [scripted drawable](/scripting/protocols/node-scripts).

When you turn on **Custom Bounds**, the region starts at the area Rive measured, so the mask looks the same until you change it.

## Resolution

**Resolution** sets how sharp the mask is, as a multiple of its size. The default is **1x**. Higher values make the mask crisper, but use more memory.
